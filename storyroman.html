The Book of Roman

Verses

A fall, a climb, a gate and a light: four chapters in verses.

Chapter 1

1:1

Long ago there lived two descendants of Adam and Eve. They were called Olha and Askold. They lived in a place very similar to Eden, but still not in it. There was a lot of everything there: food, livestock, materials for life. But neither Olha nor Askold were any longer like their ancestors. Olha was not created from Askold’s rib. Once the devil visited them and persuaded Askold to fall in love with Olha. He did so, but nothing came of it between him and her. Seeing Askold’s weakness and pliability, God ordered the devil to bind him and drag him out of the garden.

1:2

The devil came and did it. At first Askold stopped seeing in the garden what he had seen before, though the garden was the same. It turned into a curtain behind which stood something more real. Then Askold wanted to find out what stood behind it. By such actions Askold bound and twisted himself more and more. The garden had edges. Beyond them there was nothing, only the dark emptiness of the unknown. Below, somewhere in the middle between the garden and the unknown, there was a place from which danger blew. The devil dragged the bound Askold to the bottom and threw him into the abyss.

1:3

The farther he fell, the more he understood why he had felt that danger before the fall. Far away in dark clouds there were demons that lay in wait for everyone who fell downward. He had to fight them to move on. Fortunately Askold recovered very quickly, so he hoped he would be able to make it through the demons. But the evil spirits were hungry. While Askold killed demons by the hundreds, they managed to gnaw him down to the bones several times. At some point there were so many of them that they ground his bones to dust.

Chapter 2

2:1

His blood and dust seeped through the demons and passed them. Below was the place that Askold had so dreamed of reaching while he fell. What was left of him turned into fertiliser, which reached the ground under the misfortunes from above. From the heap of fertiliser grew a strong, thick grapevine. It wound around a thin, tall and hard pole.

2:2

One of the branches of the vine that grew from Askold’s dust bore a fruit. In that fruit Askold was reborn and went on being himself. An angel caught me and carried me up the mountain so that I could see what I had been so strongly drawn to when I was above.

2:3

I look around and see that I am not the only one. Every person has their own pole with a vine. It turned out that it appears at the birth of every person. A thin rod, something like the handle of a tool, stands upright beside each person. The ends of the handles are very sharp. They all have different lengths depending on the person. For some the pole is no higher than the trees, and for others it is almost infinitely high. A vine always winds around the handle. If the pole is high, the stems of the vine are usually very thick, and the other way round: if the pole is low, they are thin.

2:4

I, like all other people, was born at the foot of the pole. We eat the fruits of our own vines and of other people’s. Sometimes we can find great, tasty and juicy branches, but most often we come across either leftovers or half-eaten scraps. We have to come to terms with this. But if someone does not want to come to terms, they have to climb up. The higher they are, the better, juicier and tastier the fruits are.

2:5

I decide that I will climb up. While climbing I notice how the poles are connected to one another by the vines. I could have crossed over along one of those vines, but then I would never have reached the top. Back when I flew with the angel I noticed that the branches of the vine can lead to very different places: to paradise or to hell, or somewhere between them, depending on how lucky you are.

2:6

For most people the second scenario suits best, when you turn aside from your original path and guess at some better one. Not everyone wants to climb up. But I did. I was one of those people who want to find out what is up there. Sometimes there are also those who simply want better fruits. In general, it is for these two reasons that people decide to climb. None of us knows what awaits us at the top. All of us who dared to climb are known by a feeling. The strength of the feeling determines whether we will be able to climb to the end of our rod.

2:7

I do reach my end. I am so tired that there is no way back. That is normal, because while I was climbing the thick vine became less. One goes away, another comes, but the vine gets thinner. If before I could lean on some stem, now I am forced to hold on constantly so as not to fall. In time only my rod was left and nothing more. It is slippery, the nights are cold, there is the constant temptation that something better may be below: everything tried to stop me. I withstood all the trials, even the strong winds over which I had no influence.

2:8

And here it is, THE END, I have reached it. There is no one there, nothing there, only emptiness and silence. Around me are the same tips of handles as mine. On some of them hang impaled the bodies of people like me. There are only two ways out of there: either to fall, or to impale myself, as the others do. If I fall, I will die as rot that turns into the earth on which my descendants will later walk. It is different if I choose to impale myself. I will fall until my pierced body is stopped by a sufficiently thick vine stem. When that happens, my blood will flow down along the stems and feed them. Thanks to it the stems will grow and give more fruit. Fruit that will feed those who did not die for it.

2:9

I decide to impale myself on the rod. My blood fed the vine and it gave good fruit. My body will stay on the rod until the end of the ages. My soul was taken by the angel to heaven and left there.

Chapter 3

3:1

It will climb the wide white stairs. Seeing a great golden gate, it will pass the judgment. It will be let through, and then all that is left is to walk through the gate. But I am afraid it will stop. Seeing beyond it eternal peace and union with everything, it will be held by a heavy habit of the mortal world. I and my soul sincerely agreed to constant struggle in life. Our contest seemed so long that we had said goodbye to peace. Once it enters Paradise, there will be nothing more: no falls and rises, no misfortune and happiness, no pain and relief; my soul will never experience such things again. Life brings misfortune, but precisely because of that it could tell happiness apart, so it will be at peace. I want it to grow unused to the earthly swings of fate and to want to leave them. After the entrance, I will not exist for it, but it will still exist. I think it is worth connecting with everything through earthly feelings. It will pass through the gate and enter Paradise.

Chapter 4

4:1

Help-for-Others, seeing all evil in the devil, will go to kill him. Taking swords of hatred, she descended into hell. Having reached the devil, she will kill him. Help-for-Others will impale the former angel on her swords, lift him above herself and cut open his belly. She will gather the blood and flesh from the devil. Having drunk the blood and eaten the flesh, she will become kin to the unclean, she will turn into Desire.

In order for the desire to fulfil itself, it will go to heaven to ask for medicine. It stepped on the constellation of Aquarius to reach the right place and to share water with the thirsty. Having come to God, it asked Him:

– Give me medicine for the sick, because I want to heal them.

– Go to the garden and cook food from the fruits that I forbade to eat. The dish must be correctly prepared, correctly served and correctly eaten. If these conditions are not met, it will turn poisonous and kill those who taste it, – God answered.

Hearing Him, the desire went to the garden and picked the forbidden fruits of knowledge. It made a salad of them and served it to people. Often people did not accept the salad, because it was too dangerous, but some dared to try. After they ate the dish to the end, they became healthy and urged everyone else to taste it.

4:2

In the healed minds of people mirrors appeared. Like a crystal in the shape of a dome, a body grew around the healthy mind. The outside world fell on the crystal, as the rays of the sun fall on the surface of water. Passing through the body half-sphere, the outside light turned into the inner light of feeling. Every person must reflect the refracted light with the mirror of their mind. When the light is reflected from the mirror, it turns into the light of thoughts. To whichever part of the dome of the body the light was directed, from there the water of actions will flow, which best nourishes the fruits that people can then eat.

Not every mind knew where to reflect its light, and that is why it often suffered from hunger or thirst. The mind that did decide to find out where to shine had abundance, peace and prosperity in its life. Every dome is different: in some the light does not pass in certain places, somewhere it is curved and refracts light differently than in others. But the mind that explored it had its own, because it knew where to shine.
