Ideology

The treatise.

Thoughts and ideas: the general goal, and the place of everything in it. The text is a work in progress, written slowly, over many years.

Part one

The division of kinds

To find your way in the world, you need to define clearly what belongs to what. Like a hermit, we need many different techniques to determine movement, the movement itself and its support. People often get lost in the variety of methods that science, philosophy and religion use to solve problems. The main thing is to understand that each of them is right in its own way. It is from that “own” perspective that we must look at them when we use them.

Take the analogy of climbing a ladder. Then the goal is the position of the body, the accomplished fact of having climbed up. The method is the way of climbing: straight up, upside down, or sideways (it all depends on wishes and ingenuity). The position in which we stand at the bottom of the ladder we will call the initial state.

Science dictates the methods of climbing to the goal, because it is the reflection of reality in ideas. We want to carry what is not real (our desire) over into what is real. Setting the goal is the job of philosophy. It determines where and how it is best to have the goal. Where we are, we learn immediately after we accept that any state (in the direction we want) will be better than the state now. In general, this position rests on the thought that the goal can be known. The best method of knowing that goal is philosophy. That is, philosophy is the method of achieving the setting of a goal.

In all this variety, religion is missing. It has no place anywhere. We have forgotten that a human is first of all an animal, and we are guided by feelings, not by reason. Our reason just formulates our feelings so that they become logical. The feelings themselves are a manifestation of God, but that is already mixing the topics. After understanding the present state, defining goals and methods of reaching them, we need to form a system in which actions are tied to emotions. Fortunately, such a system can be universal. So religion (or belief) is the method for following the clear rules set by reason.

In short

Science is the method, philosophy sets the goal, and religion ties actions to emotion.

Part two

Two ways of reasoning

Because faith and knowledge have different foundations, which are examined below, we must be able to switch between a state in which we are guided by knowledge and a state in which we are guided by faith. There is no better way to do this than to understand clearly the difference between them.

The essence of reasoning by means of available knowledge is the idea that everything can be understood, and that the knowledge obtained can be used in the future to get the desired result. To gain some knowledge, we have to understand two states of action, in which we either act according to it or do not. For example, getting up early. We know that it is better to get up early, so we can either get up early or get up late. If we get up early and do not feel better, then the knowledge is not true (it does not always work this simply; this example is a simplification). Knowledge does not give meaning, because it is only methods for solving individual tasks; setting the task itself is another matter. Reasoning through knowledge is hard. It is hard to use, because it demands rebellion against your own values in order to test whether they are correct. To get real knowledge we often have to act against what we think is right, in order to really find out whether it is so.

The essence of reasoning by means of faith is the idea that tradition, which was selected by natural selection, is a better source of knowledge than our local reasoning at the level of one life. At best, this approach is extended by the possibility of adding new traditions, but often people hold to one and do not change it.

In short

Knowledge asks you to rebel against your own values to test them. Faith trusts the tradition that survived selection.

03

Religion

God

To understand who God is, we have to define his features. He certainly is: everywhere, alive, just and good. The essence of the God we are talking about is connection. Wherever we can find connection, there is God too. If we do not find connection, then God is not there either. A direct example of God’s manifestation is interaction with the surrounding world: if you push a small stone, it will roll. If God is connection, and wherever there is connection there is also God, then the connection of cause and effect is one of the Gods.

God comes in different kinds. There are two different ways of describing him: a description through his action and a description through his definition. A description through action presents God as living or written, and as old or new. A description of God through definition gives four figures:

  • the one who creates but was not created;
  • the one who was created and creates;
  • the one who does not create but was created;
  • the one who was not created and does not create.
In short

God is connection, and the connection of cause and effect is one of the Gods.

04

Religion

The material world

The material world is one of the four figures of God. It is the God who was created but does not create. This part was created by another, namely by the God who was created and creates (science).

Setting a goal in such a world is a problem, because even after reaching it, if that ever happens, neither pure happiness nor pure peace will come. This is so because the material world is not perfect: it is an averaging. If the quintessence is averaging, then to be the best manifestation of such a world you have to be averaged, and so never fully what you really want.

One should treat the material world with condescension. This is how it should be seen, because you will never fully realise your desire. So to get happiness and peace after all, you can point out its problems by way of ideality: a rebellion against the world itself, through using it in the best possible way.

In short

The material world is only an average. Treat it with condescension and use it in the best possible way.

05

Religion

The world of science

The world of science is one of the four figures of God. It is the God who was created and creates. This world creates the material one. Its laws govern everything that exists in reality. For example, Newton’s law is more real than the stone lying under our feet. Science was created by another part of God: the one who was not created, but creates.

In short

Science is created and creates: it makes the material world, and its laws are more real than the stone.
